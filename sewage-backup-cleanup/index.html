Emergency Sewage Backup Cleanup in Loma Linda, CA

Sewage that comes back up a drain is contaminated water, and it needs containment before anything else. Dispatch answers around the clock.

☎ Call (909) 505-3870

Sewage coming up through a floor drain, tub or toilet is an emergency, and Loma Linda Water Damage Pros runs 24/7 dispatch for it. While you wait, stop using every toilet, sink, shower and washing machine in the house, keep children and pets out of the wet area, and lift dry belongings out of reach. Do not try to flush or plunge the problem away.

Sewage backup cleanup is the work of containing, removing and disinfecting wastewater that has come back up a drain line into a home in Loma Linda, CA, then drying whatever can be saved. It is Category 3 water under ANSI/IICRC S500, a published industry standard worth asking any company about. The job is about contamination first and moisture second.

Sewage is coming up right now: what to do first

  1. Stop running water anywhere in the house, because every drain feeds the same blocked line.
  2. Keep everyone out of the affected rooms, and close the door if the room has one.
  3. Photograph the water line and the damaged items before anything is moved.
  4. Call a plumber for the line and call dispatch for the contaminated water, because both jobs are needed.

Power and contact warning

If sewage is touching outlets, a power strip or the electrical panel, do not reach into it. Cut power at the panel only if the panel is dry and you can get to it without stepping in the water. If you smell gas or see sparking, leave and call 911 or the utility before you call anyone else.

City sewer main or your own line

City Public Works in Loma Linda lists water and sewer together and posts a Sewer Emergency Response Plan on its water and sewer page. That tells you who to call for a problem in the street. If sewage is surfacing outside, or neighbors have the same trouble, report it to the city. If only your house is affected, the blockage is more likely in your own lateral, and a plumber is the right first call.

Either way, neither the city nor the plumber removes the contaminated water from your floors. That is a restoration crew's job, so book both and tell each one what the other is doing.

Safety gear for sewage

The CDC says anyone dealing with sewage should wear rubber boots, rubber gloves and goggles, wash cleanup clothing in hot water and detergent apart from other laundry, and wash any open cut with soap and apply antibiotic ointment. Better still, leave the wet area alone and let trained workers in full protective gear handle it.

How the crew contains, removes and disinfects

Work starts with containment. Crews hang plastic barriers over doorways and vents so contaminated air and foot traffic do not reach clean rooms. HEPA air scrubbers run during removal, and extraction units pull standing water into sealed equipment. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras then show how far the water traveled into walls and flooring.

Next comes removal of anything porous that the water soaked, then cleaning and disinfecting of hard surfaces and framing. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the structure afterward, and readings are taken each visit until the materials reach their drying goal.

What gets thrown away and why

The CDC lists carpeting, padding, rugs, upholstered furniture, mattresses and paper products as items to discard when they cannot be cleaned and dried quickly. It also says drywall and insulation contaminated with sewage should be removed. So wet carpet and pad from a backup normally head to the dumpster. Photograph everything first, since your insurer may ask what was lost.

Never pump it outside

The city's Storm Water page explains that ditches and storm drains are not connected to the sewer system and flow directly to waterways. It lists dumping household wastewater into them as an illegal discharge. So do not pump or hose contaminated water out to the gutter or the street.

Why a backup cannot sit

County Environmental Health Services says sewage that surfaces or backs up into a home is a health hazard that must be eliminated as quickly as possible. Its guidance is written mostly around septic systems, so it speaks most directly to unsewered county areas. At a sewered Loma Linda address you start with the city and a plumber, but standing sewage still soaks into drywall and subfloor and does not get cleaner with time.

What changes the cost of sewage cleanup

  • The area and depth of the spill, meaning how many rooms and how much flooring were reached.
  • The materials involved, since carpet, pad, drywall and insulation are removed while tile and concrete are cleaned.
  • Containment and air scrubbing needs, which grow with the size of the affected zone.
  • Drying days and equipment count after the cleanup is done.
  • Rebuild scope, which covers replacing what was removed and is quoted apart from the emergency work.

Insurance can shift who pays for what. Ask for your real number by calling dispatch, where the scope is explained before work begins.

Insurance and sewer backup coverage

A backup is not the same event as a burst pipe, and policies can treat the two differently. Ask your insurer whether a water or sewer backup endorsement is on your policy, since that coverage is often an optional add on. Check your policy and keep photos and receipts. Renters should tell the landlord in writing right away.

Mistakes that make a backup worse

  • Running the washer or dishwasher to see if the drain has cleared.
  • Mixing bleach with other cleaners, since bleach and ammonia make dangerous fumes.
  • Waiting a day to see if it drains, while porous materials keep absorbing.

Sewage backup questions

Is emergency sewage backup cleanup available at night or on weekends in Loma Linda?

Yes. Dispatch runs 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Call, say which fixtures are backing up and whether water has reached outlets, and dispatch will tell you what to do while a crew is arranged.

Should I call the plumber or the restoration crew first?

Call both. The plumber clears or repairs the line so it stops backing up. The restoration crew removes the contaminated water and cleans what it touched. Neither can do the other's job.

Can I clean up a small sewage spill myself?

A tiny spill on a hard floor can be handled with gloves, boots, goggles and a disinfectant used as directed. Anything that reached carpet, baseboards or walls is a professional job.

Why does carpet get thrown out after a backup?

Carpet and pad soak up contaminated water and cannot be reliably disinfected all the way through. Cutting them out stops odor and further spread.

How long does drying take after the cleanup?

Usually three to five days, longer for plaster, thick insulation or hardwood. Readings guide when the equipment comes out.
