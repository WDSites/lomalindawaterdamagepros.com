One City Answers for Your Water in Colton

Colton is unusual in the Inland Empire because the City of Colton runs its own water, wastewater and electric utility. Its water comes from 10 wells and 6 reservoirs, including the 2 million gallon Wild Canyon Reservoir, and the system includes over 120 miles of pipe. For a homeowner with a leak, that means one city department answers for both the water and the sewer.

The electric utility is also the city's, which matters when water reaches outlets or a panel. Call the utility or 911 for any electrical risk before you call anyone else. We provide 24/7 emergency dispatch for Colton from our Loma Linda office, and we start with the water, not the wiring.

Where a leak sits decides who fixes it. A break on the street side of the meter belongs to the city, and a break on the house side belongs to you. If you cannot tell which side is failing, ask the city before you dig or pay for a repair. The same city wastewater plant also serves Grand Terrace, so neighbors up the hill share part of the system with you.

When Water Is Spreading in Colton

If water is moving across a floor right now, stop the source first. Close the main valve at the house, or the small shutoff under a sink or behind a washer. Then call our emergency line, any hour, and tell the dispatcher what you see and which cross street you are nearest.

Crews pull standing water with pumps and extraction units, which is the work described on our emergency water removal page. A clean supply line break can turn contaminated if it sits, so speed matters. We will not promise an arrival time, because traffic and other calls decide that.

Shut off the water

Find the main valve at the house and turn it clockwise. If the leak is a failed city main or a meter problem, call the city water line.

Protect the power

Cut power to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you can reach it from dry ground. Otherwise stay out and call the utility or 911.

Lift what you can

Move shoes, papers, electronics and small furniture onto dry counters or beds. Pull rugs off wet floors and keep pets out of the water.

Note your access

Tell dispatch your nearest cross street and where to park. Trains and freeway ramps can slow a truck in Colton, and a good landmark saves minutes.

Getting Around a City Cut by Rails and Freeways

I-10 and I-215 both cross Colton, and rail corridors run through the city as well. Colton Crossing, the rail flyover that opened in 2013, lifted one busy junction, but a stopped freight train can still close a street crossing. Because of that, give dispatch a cross street and a second route if you know one.

Colton sits west of Loma Linda, roughly 3 to 4 miles away. The same line that covers Grand Terrace and San Bernardino covers you. We do not quote drive times.

Permits, Maps and Old Floods

Development Services, which includes Building and Safety, handles permits in Colton and runs an online portal. If your repair means opening walls, moving plumbing or rewiring, ask Development Services whether it needs a permit. Do that before the rebuild starts, not after.

The city also posts FEMA maps and its Local Hazard Mitigation Plan on its Public Works pages. Check the FEMA map for your address, since flood exposure differs from street to street. A standard homeowners policy does not cover flood, so ask your insurer about separate flood coverage.

The region has seen this before. In January 1943 the Lytle Creek flood forced 150 families out of their homes in San Bernardino and Colton, according to the National Weather Service. The practical lesson is to know where water goes on your lot, keep gutters and drains clear before a storm, and keep your insurance papers reachable. A flooded home falls under flood damage restoration.

What We Do After the Water Is Out

Pumping removes the standing water, but walls, subfloors and framing still hold moisture. Our crews use moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras to find it, then set air movers and LGR dehumidifiers to dry the structure. This is water mitigation, and it usually takes three to five days, longer for hardwood, plaster or thick insulation.

Wet drywall, pad and damaged contents are removed under water damage cleanup. Drying wet materials within about 24 to 48 hours helps prevent mold. Ask any company whether its crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500, and check its license on the CSLB site yourself.

Colton Water Damage Questions

Do you cover every ZIP code in Colton?

Colton uses a single ZIP, 92324, and we cover all of it, from the downtown streets near the rail lines to the neighborhoods along the I-10 and I-215 corridors.

Part of Grand Terrace also uses 92324, so some addresses with a Colton mailing line are actually in Grand Terrace. We serve those too, and the dispatcher only needs the street address and a cross street.

Is emergency water removal available in Colton?

Yes. Dispatch runs around the clock, including nights, weekends and holidays. Tell the dispatcher where the water is coming from and whether anything electrical is wet. We do not promise a set arrival time.

Who do I call for a water main break versus a house line leak?

Water on the street side of the meter is the city's to repair, so call the City of Colton water department. A leak after the meter is yours, and you can close your own main valve. A restoration crew cannot repair a city main, but we can dry what the water reached.

What if sewage is backing up into my house?

Stay out, keep children and pets away, and do not run water or flush. Sewage is contaminated Category 3 water and needs protective gear. Call us for extraction and cleanup, and call the city if the problem looks like the street main.

Does water damage repair in Colton need a permit?

It depends on the work. Drying alone often does not, but opening walls or changing plumbing or wiring might. Ask Development Services, and use its online portal for permit questions.

Does homeowners insurance cover a flood in Colton?

A standard homeowners policy generally does not cover flood, and flood coverage is a separate policy. Sudden water damage, such as a burst pipe, is often covered. Check your policy, keep receipts for emergency steps, and ask your insurer before permanent repairs.
