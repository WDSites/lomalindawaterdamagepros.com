Creek channels and storms: how water gets into San Bernardino buildings

Several creeks drain toward the Santa Ana River through the city, including City Creek, Lytle Creek, San Timoteo Creek, Twin Creek and Warm Creek, and many of them run in flood control channels. If your street sits near one of these creek channels, look up your address on the FEMA flood map before a storm instead of after it.

County archives document a March 1938 flood, and the regional Lytle Creek flood of January 1943 evacuated 150 families in San Bernardino and Colton. The regional storms of January 2023 put runoff in normally dry channels in San Bernardino. Clear gutters and yard drains before a forecast, not during it.

Stormwater is contaminated. It carries soil, bacteria and debris, which makes it Category 3 water under ANSI/IICRC S500, so wet carpet, pad and drywall are usually removed instead of dried. Flood is excluded from standard homeowners policies, and flood coverage is separate. National Flood Insurance Program policies usually take effect 30 days after purchase, so read your policy now. When water is spreading in San Bernardino

Water moving across a floor is an emergency, whatever its source. Storm runoff, a failed supply line and a sewage backup all get worse by the hour, and dispatch answers 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Emergency water removal starts with extraction units pulling the standing water. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras then show what is still wet behind the walls.

San Bernardino is a big city, so dispatch asks for the street address and the nearest cross street on every call. The city has many ZIP codes, and one street can sit close to a boundary. Give the address first and ask dispatch to confirm your ZIP.

Before we arrive

Find the main valve

If a supply line or water heater is the source, shut off the main water valve at the house. If the water is coming in from the street or a creek channel, you cannot stop it, so move people and pets to higher ground.

Check the panel before you touch it

Cut power to the wet area only if the breaker panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. If water is touching outlets or you smell gas, call the utility or 911 first.

Lift, then photograph

Take photos of the water line and the damage, then lift electronics, papers and small furniture onto dry surfaces. Keep receipts for emergency protection costs, because the insurer reimburses reasonable ones.

Keep out of dark water

Keep children and pets out of storm water and anything that smells like sewage. Have your street address, cross street and a plain description of the water ready for dispatch.

Who to call besides us: the water department and the permit office

The San Bernardino municipal water department, established in 1905, provides water and sewer service. If the problem is the sewer main in the street, call the municipal water department. If sewage is coming up inside your house, call a plumber and a restoration crew at once, because the plumber clears the line and the crew cleans and dries what the sewage touched.

The East Valley Water District, covered on our Highland page, also serves parts of San Bernardino. Check your water bill to see which agency is yours before you call the wrong one.

For permits, Building and Safety sits within Community Development and Housing at 201 North E Street, 3rd Floor. Ask whether your repair needs a permit, and confirm office hours before you drive over.

San Bernardino water damage questions

Is emergency water removal available in San Bernardino?

Yes. Dispatch runs 24/7, so a call at 2 a.m. on a holiday gets answered. Tell them the source, whether sewage or electrical risk is involved, and the street address. Crews are sent as quickly as conditions allow, and dispatch tells you what to do while you wait.

Does homeowners insurance cover flooding from a creek channel?

Usually not. Flood is excluded from standard homeowners policies and needs separate flood coverage, which typically starts 30 days after purchase. A sudden burst pipe is often treated differently. Read your policy and ask your insurer.

Who handles a sewer main problem versus a backup in my house?

A problem in the sewer main under the street belongs to the municipal water department, which provides sewer service. A backup that reaches your floor drains or toilets is a job for a plumber and a restoration crew.

Sewage is Category 3 water, so stay out of the room until a crew is on site.

My street is next to a creek channel. What should I do before a storm?

Look up your address on the FEMA flood map, check what your policy covers and clear gutters and yard drains. Keep the main valve location and our dispatch line in mind. Photographing your rooms beforehand makes a later claim easier.
