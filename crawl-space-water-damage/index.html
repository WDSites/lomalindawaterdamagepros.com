Standing water under a house is an emergency, and Loma Linda Water Damage Pros runs 24/7 dispatch for it. While you wait, close the main water valve if a supply line is the suspect, keep the access hatch shut, and keep children and pets away from it. Do not climb down to look.

Crawl space water damage is the wetting of floor joists, subfloor, insulation, vapor barrier and pipe wrapping in the low, unfinished area beneath a raised floor in a Loma Linda, CA home. Our crews pump out the water, remove ruined material and dry the framing. A plumber or drainage contractor fixes the source. Many local homes have no crawl space at all, so check whether yours has an access hatch or foundation vents.

Water under the floor right now: what to do first

  • Shut the main valve if the water looks like it comes from a pipe, then see whether the meter still moves.
  • Stay out of the crawl space. You cannot see wiring, and you may not be able to back out quickly.
  • Photograph what you can from the hatch opening, plus stained ceilings and floors above.
  • Lift rugs and furniture legs off floors that feel spongy or cold.
  • Call a plumber for the source and call dispatch for pumping and drying.

Crawl space safety: tight quarters, wiring and old insulation

Why we ask you not to go under

Wiring, junction boxes and sometimes ducts run through crawl spaces, and standing water can reach them. Turn off power to the affected area only at a panel that is dry and easy to reach. If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 and the gas utility before you call dispatch.

Census ACS 2023 estimates say 1,318 Loma Linda housing units were built before 1960, and 564 of those date from 1939 or earlier. About one unit in three, 32.6 percent, was built before 1980. The Contractors State License Board says pipe insulation made from 1920 to 1972 and wall and ceiling insulation from 1930 to 1950 can contain asbestos. You cannot tell by looking, so test before anyone tears wrapped pipe or old batting out.

How water gets under a house

A broken supply line, a leaking drain, an overflowing water heater pan or an air conditioner condensate line can all drip into the space. Rain runoff can also enter at vents when yard grading sends water toward the foundation. The Redlands climate station records most of its rain from December through February, so a problem under the house often shows itself in the first heavy storm of the season.

How the crew pumps out and dries the space

Crews enter in protective gear and start with extraction, using submersible pumps and wet vacuums to take standing water out of the space. Soaked batting and damaged vapor barrier are bagged and removed, once asbestos concerns have been addressed. Dirty water from a drain or from outside is treated as contaminated, and the crew changes the plan to match.

Drying follows. Air movers push air across joists and the underside of the subfloor, LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture from the space, and moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera track progress from above. Drying usually takes three to five days, longer for soaked framing or thick insulation. Our emergency water removal page covers pump-outs of every size, and water mitigation explains the first days of a dry out.

Why the 24 to 48 hour clock matters

The CDC says that if a home and its contents cannot be dried within 24 to 48 hours, you should assume mold is growing. The EPA says materials left wet for more than two days usually get moldy. Wet insulation under the floor is the same problem in a place you cannot see. If growth is found, our mold remediation page explains the next step.

What changes the cost of crawl space work

  • Access and clearance, since a tight hatch and low height slow every task.
  • Water category, because dirty water means more removal and more protection.
  • Depth and area of the standing water.
  • Insulation, vapor barrier and pipe wrap that must come out, and any testing first.
  • Drying days and the amount of equipment the framing needs.

Insurance fit under a raised floor

A burst pipe is sudden and often covered, while slow seepage over many months often is not. Water that rises from rain outside is a flood question, and flood is usually excluded from standard homeowners policies. Check your policy, ask your insurer which applies, and keep damaged material for the adjuster.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Going under the house to look while wiring is wet.
  • Pulling out old pipe wrap or insulation without testing first.
  • Closing the vents and hoping the space dries on its own.
  • Laying new flooring before moisture readings are done.
  • Skipping the plumber or drainage fix, so the space floods again.

Crawl space water damage questions

Is emergency crawl space water cleanup available at night or weekends in Loma Linda?

Yes. Dispatch runs 24/7, including holidays. Describe what you can see from the hatch, and a crew is sent as conditions allow. No arrival time is promised.

How do I know if my home has a crawl space?

Look for a small access hatch in a closet or hallway floor, or for vents along the base of the exterior walls. If neither exists, your home may sit on a slab, and the slab leak page may apply.

Can I go down and check the damage myself?

We advise against it. Wiring, low clearance and possible old insulation make it unsafe, and a crew has the protective gear and lighting.

Will you fix the drainage so it does not flood again?

No. We remove water and dry the structure. A plumber fixes pipes, and a licensed contractor handles grading and drainage.

Is the old insulation safe to remove?

Do not assume. The Contractors State License Board says you cannot tell asbestos by looking. Ask about testing before anyone disturbs it.

Does insurance pay for crawl space water damage?

It depends on the cause and the policy. Sudden pipe failures are often treated differently from seepage or rising runoff, so ask your insurer.
