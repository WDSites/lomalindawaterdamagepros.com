If Water Is Still Spreading, Start Here

These four steps protect your home and your insurance claim while you wait for a crew. Do only what is safe, and skip any step that puts you near live electricity or contaminated water.

1. Stop the source

Close the main water valve at your house if you can reach it. For a burst supply line, the shutoff under the sink or behind the toilet often stops the leak faster than the main.

2. Make the room safe

Turn off power to wet areas only when the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water. If water is near outlets, a panel or an appliance, stay out and call 911 or the utility first.

3. Lift and protect

Move rugs, electronics, paper and small furniture to a dry room. Keep children and pets away from standing water, and treat any water from a toilet, drain or storm as contaminated.

4. Document, then call

Take photos and a short video of the damage before you move things, keep receipts for emergency costs, and call dispatch. A crew can take it from there at any hour.

Water Categories at a Glance

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard groups water by contamination level, and the category decides what can be dried in place and what must be removed. Ask any restoration company whether its crews work to S500.

Water categories under S500
CategoryTypical sourceWhat usually happens
Category 1Broken supply line, sink overflow with clean water, rain through a roof leakExtraction and drying in place when caught early
Category 2Dishwasher or washing machine discharge, aquarium, toilet overflow without solidsExtraction, removal of porous materials such as carpet pad, cleaning and drying
Category 3Sewage, river or storm floodwater, long-standing waterContainment, removal of affected materials, sanitizing, then drying

Clean water can slip into a higher category when it sits. A Category 1 leak that soaks carpet for several days can be treated as Category 2 or 3, which is one more reason to call early.

What Affects the Cost of Water Damage Restoration

No honest company can price a wet house over the phone. These five factors set the number, and your assessment shows each one in writing before work starts.

  • The water category, which decides how much has to be removed and sanitized.
  • The affected area, counted in rooms, floors and wall height rather than a single square footage.
  • The materials involved, such as carpet, hardwood, drywall, plaster or insulation.
  • Drying days and equipment, because more air movers and dehumidifiers running longer cost more.
  • The rebuild scope, which depends on what was removed and what you want restored.

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by a homeowners or renters policy, slow leaks and neglect often are not, and flood usually needs separate flood coverage. Read your policy or ask your insurer before you decide. Want your real number? Call dispatch and describe the damage.

Documenting a loss for your insurer

Photograph every wet surface before anything is moved, list damaged items, keep receipts for emergency work, and avoid extensive permanent repairs until the adjuster has inspected. For larger losses, ask for the drying log and moisture readings, which show what was wet and when it dried.

Mold After a Leak

Mold needs moisture, and wet drywall, carpet pad and insulation that stay damp for more than a day or two give it that moisture. If you see spotting on a ceiling, a closet wall or a bathroom corner, fix the water source first and then call about mold remediation. Questions about testing belong with an independent tester, and health questions belong with your physician.

Water Damage Questions We Hear in Loma Linda

Do you handle water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Loma Linda?

Yes. The dispatch line is answered 24 hours a day, every day of the year, and after-hours calls get the same questions and the same instructions as daytime calls. Same-day response is offered when crews are available, and dispatch tells you what is open when you call.

How fast can someone come out?

We do not promise an arrival time, because traffic, the size of the job and crew availability change by the hour. Dispatch gives you a plain answer on the phone and tells you what to do while you wait.

Does water damage restoration fix the cause, like foundation waterproofing?

No. Restoration dries and repairs what the water damaged, and it is not waterproofing. If water keeps entering through a foundation or a grade problem, you also need the right trade for that, and we will tell you so.

Will my insurance pay for water damage restoration?

It depends on your policy and the cause. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and keep photos, receipts and drying records.

Do I need a permit for water damage repairs?

Drying and cleanup usually are not the issue, but rebuilding can be. Ask the Loma Linda Building and Safety Division at the city Community Development Department whether your repair needs a permit.

How long does drying take?

Many jobs dry in about three to five days, and thick materials such as hardwood, plaster or heavy insulation take longer. Crews check moisture readings each visit and remove equipment only when readings meet the drying goal.

What happens with a large loss or a claim that needs documentation?

For a large loss we document affected rooms, materials and readings so your adjuster can see the scope. Ask for the drying log when the job ends, and keep it with your photos.
