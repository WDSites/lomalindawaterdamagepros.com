The city lists eight local historic districts, including Eureka Street, West Highland Avenue, Early Redlands, Normandie Court, East Fern Avenue, Garden Hill, La Verne Street and Smiley Park, plus two National Register districts at the Santa Fe Depot and Smiley Park.

Drying is not the same as preservation review

In the city's historic districts, exterior changes need a Certificate of Appropriateness. Pulling water out of walls and drying a floor from the inside is a different matter. Before you rebuild anything visible from the street, ask the city's One Stop Permit Center what review applies.

When water is spreading in Redlands

A burst supply line or a failed water heater does not wait for business hours. Our 24/7 emergency line is staffed so you can describe what is happening and get dispatch started. We never promise an arrival time, but we do tell you what to do while you wait.

Emergency water removal is available in Redlands at night and on weekends, because plaster and old subfloors soak up water fast.

Stop the water

Find the main shutoff and close it. In an older home it may be stiff or buried behind shrubs, so locate it before an emergency.

Cut power only if safe

If water is near outlets or the panel, stay out. Switch off power to wet areas only from a dry spot, and call the utility or 911 for any electrical risk.

Protect what you can lift

Raise rugs, books, shoes and framed pieces onto dry surfaces. Leave antique furniture and plaster details for the crew.

Do not scrub or demolish

Skip pulling down plaster or sealing a wet room. Take photos, note the source, and let emergency dispatch guide the next step.

Services for Redlands homes, in order of need

In older houses, the repair side is where most Redlands calls end up. Water damage repair covers plaster patching, drywall, flooring and paint once the structure reads dry, and in a historic home that scope is worth discussing before work begins.

Before any rebuild comes water mitigation, which means extraction, moisture meters, thermal imaging cameras, air movers and LGR dehumidifiers. Plaster and hardwood usually need longer than the usual three to five days of drying.

Afterward, water damage cleanup handles contents, sanitizing and odor. When the water came from a washer or dishwasher, that step matters even if the room looks fine.

Who to call for water, sewer and storm drains

The city's Municipal Utilities and Engineering Department runs Redlands water, sewer and storm drains. If you see a main leaking in the street or a sewer problem outside your property line, call the city department. Water that is already inside your house is ours and your plumber's job.

A sewage backup inside the home is Category 3 water. Keep people and pets out, and call a plumber and our emergency line. Historic Mill Creek Zanja canal now carries drainage and storm runoff, so ask the city about any drainage near your lot.

Owners, renters and insurance in Redlands

About 57.4 percent of Redlands homes are owner occupied across roughly 25,400 households, which leaves a large share of renters. Tenants should report a leak to the landlord in writing, and landlords should settle who files the claim before drying starts.

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks often are not. Flood is usually excluded from standard homeowners policies. Check your policy, keep receipts, and do not make permanent repairs before the adjuster inspects.

Can you dry a historic Redlands home without damaging it?

Interior drying uses moisture meters, thermal imaging cameras and controlled air movement, and plaster can be dried with patience. Ask the crew to explain what comes out and what stays before work begins. Exterior changes are a separate matter for the city.

Does my historic district home need a Certificate of Appropriateness for water repairs?

The city says exterior changes in its historic districts need one. Interior drying is separate. Ask the One Stop Permit Center before you rebuild anything visible from the street, and keep photos of the damage.

Who handles a water main, a sewer main or a backup?

The city's Municipal Utilities and Engineering Department handles water, sewer and storm drains in the street. A backup inside the house means calling a plumber and our emergency line, since sewage water needs careful removal.

Should I call before or after my insurer?

Stop the leak and call us first, then your insurer. The Department of Insurance says to dry promptly and keep receipts for emergency protection. Do not make extensive permanent repairs until the adjuster inspects.
