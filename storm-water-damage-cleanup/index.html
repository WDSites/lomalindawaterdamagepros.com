Storm water damage cleanup in Loma Linda, CA is the work of extracting rain, runoff and mud from a building, then cleaning, disinfecting and drying what the water touched. Water that has crossed soil or a street is treated as Category 3 under ANSI/IICRC S500, a published industry standard to ask any company about.

Storm safety on the street and at home

Moving water is dangerous

The National Weather Service says 6 inches of fast moving water can knock down an adult and 12 inches can carry away most cars. Do not drive or wade through flooded roads to reach your home. If water is around your electrical panel, stay out and call the utility or 911 first.

Where storm water gets into a home

Most storm intrusion starts small. Rain finds a worn roof section, a door threshold with a poor seal, a garage door gap or a window frame. Yard water that has nowhere to go runs toward the lowest opening.

A roof entry often travels along rafters before it stains a ceiling, so the damp spot is not directly under the hole. Our roof leak water damage page covers that path in detail.

What the crew does once it is safe to enter

The crew starts with a walkthrough using moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera, then sets up the job. Extraction units remove standing water and mud, and wet carpet, pad and soaked insulation are cut out if they cannot be cleaned. Surfaces are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products.

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers then dry the structure, with readings taken each day. HEPA air scrubbers run where dust or contaminated material was disturbed. Drying is usually three to five days, longer for hardwood, plaster or thick insulation.

Storm intrusion versus rising flood on your policy

California Department of Insurance guidance describes a flood under the national program as temporary inundation of at least two acres of normally dry land, or of two or more properties, from overflow, runoff or mudflow. Water entering one house from a leaking roof is usually a different kind of loss. Describe exactly what happened to your insurer, check your policy and ask whether you carry separate flood coverage.

What changes the cost of storm cleanup

  • Water category, since runoff and mud need disinfecting and more material removal.
  • Affected area, meaning how many rooms and whether a garage or lower level is wet.
  • Materials, because carpet, pad and drywall are often replaced while concrete and solid wood can be dried.
  • Drying days and equipment, which depend on how long the water stood.
  • Rebuild scope after drying, which is quoted separately.

Ask dispatch for your real number. Insurance may change who pays what, so check your policy first.

Storm water damage questions

Is emergency storm water damage cleanup available at night or weekends in Loma Linda?

Yes. Dispatch answers 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Tell them where water is entering and whether power or sewage is involved, and they will guide you on what to do next.

Is storm water treated as clean water?

No. Once rain crosses soil, a street or a yard, it can carry bacteria and debris. Crews treat it as Category 3 and usually discard wet carpet pad and soaked insulation.

Will you fix my roof?

No. A roofer handles tarping and repair. We extract water, dry the ceiling and attic insulation and clean up the interior.

Can I clean mud out myself?

Small amounts, yes, with boots and gloves. Do not wash it into sinks or storm drains. Large amounts or mud mixed with sewage should go to a crew.

Should I wait for the storm to end before calling?

No. Call as soon as water is inside, because wet materials start to deteriorate quickly. Dispatch will tell you what is safe to do while the weather is still active.
