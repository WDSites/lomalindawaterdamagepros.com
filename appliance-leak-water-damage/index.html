How appliance leaks start

Most appliance floods are small failures that keep running. A washing machine hose splits under constant pressure. A dishwasher door seal loses grip, or its drain hose kinks behind the unit. A disposal connection loosens until it drips into the cabinet. A refrigerator ice maker line can also weep behind the unit for weeks.

The slow ones are the sneaky ones. Water soaks the subfloor and the wall base where nobody looks, so the first clue may be a swollen baseboard or a soft patch of flooring.

Why this water is not clean

Under ANSI/IICRC S500, a published industry standard to ask about, washer and dishwasher discharge is Category 2 water. It carries detergent, food residue, lint and bacteria. That changes the job. Carpet and pad that soaked in it are usually removed, while hard floors can be cleaned and dried in place.

A supply line break starts as Category 1 but can slip toward Category 2 if it sits in carpet or insulation. Do not wait to see which it is.

Safety for the first moments after a leak

  • Close the shutoff valves behind the appliance. If they are stuck, close the main water valve for the home.
  • Unplug the appliance only if the outlet and floor around it are dry.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Lift rugs, laundry baskets and cardboard boxes off the wet floor.
  • If water is near an outlet, a panel or a light fixture, stay out and call an electrician before dispatch.

Apartments, upstairs units and shared walls

According to Census data, only 48.7 percent of Loma Linda housing units are single family detached houses. That leaves many households sharing a wall, floor or ceiling with someone else. A washer leak on a second floor can reach a downstairs unit through light fixtures and ceiling seams.

If that is your building, tell the neighbor below and the property manager early. Photograph both sides of the damage, and take a short video of the ceiling before anyone cuts into it.

Renters: who to tell and when

About 60 percent of Loma Linda households rent, so many leaks happen in a home the resident does not own. A California Superior Court tenant handout says to tell the landlord when a unit needs repair, preferably right away by phone and in writing. It warns that delay can cause more damage and that the tenant may be held responsible for the extra harm.

That makes a fast text or email the safest first move. Keep a copy, and add photos of the appliance and the wet floor.

Another court handout says a landlord normally gives 24 hours of written notice before entering, but the notice rule does not apply in an emergency such as a broken water pipe. Expect plumbers and a drying crew to need quick access, and tell the property manager who you called.

Which insurance responds

The California Department of Insurance says a landlord does not insure a tenant's personal property. Renters policies generally cover belongings, loss of use, liability and medical payments, with loss of use typically 20 percent of the personal property limit. The landlord's policy may rebuild the walls, but it does not replace your couch or clothing.

For owners, sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, and slow leaks often are not. Check your policy and ask your insurer, and keep the failed hose or part until the adjuster has seen it.

How the crew dries it out

Extraction units pull water off hard floors and out of carpet first. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras then follow the water into cabinet bases, wall bottoms and the floor beneath. Category 2 carpet pad is lifted and discarded, and affected surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment.

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run for several days. Drying usually takes three to five days, longer for hardwood or plaster. Crews check readings daily, and a leak that crossed into a ceiling below is handled the same way on that side. Our water mitigation page explains the early stages.

What changes the cost

  • The water category, since Category 2 needs more cleaning and removal.
  • How many floors or units the water reached.
  • Flooring type, because carpet and pad are removed while tile dries in place.
  • Drying days and equipment count.
  • Rebuild scope, such as cabinet bases, drywall and paint, which is quoted separately.

Insurance may change who pays for what. Ask dispatch for your real number, and the scope is explained before work begins.

Prevention and mistakes to avoid

Look behind your washer and dishwasher at hoses and shutoff valves a few times a year. Replace hoses that are bulged, cracked or rusted at the fittings. Never run a washer while you are out of the house.

Do not hide a wet floor under a new mat, and do not close up a cabinet base that still feels damp. For broader spills, see our water damage cleanup page. We also serve Highland homes and San Bernardino apartments.

Appliance leak questions

Is emergency appliance leak cleanup available at night or on weekends in Loma Linda?

Yes. Dispatch runs 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Call and say which appliance leaked and whether water reached a neighbor.

Is washing machine water dangerous to leave on the floor?

It is Category 2 water, so it carries bacteria and should not sit in carpet. Extract it promptly and keep children and pets off the area.

Will you repair the washer or dishwasher?

No. An appliance technician or plumber fixes the unit and its lines. We dry and clean the structure.

My upstairs neighbor's washer leaked into my ceiling. Who pays?

That depends on the cause, the lease and the policies involved. Notify your landlord in writing, document everything and ask your insurer.

Can the carpet be saved?

Sometimes. Category 2 pad is usually discarded, and carpet that stayed wet for long is often removed too. Moisture readings decide.
