Hardwood Floor Repair After Water Damage in Loma Linda

By the Loma Linda Water Damage Pros team

Wet hardwood often looks worse than it is, and it can look fine while it is still wet. Wait for the readings before you decide.

Can wet hardwood be saved

Often, yes, if the water was clean, the leak was caught within a few days, and drying starts quickly. Solid hardwood can swell, cup and recover when moisture leaves slowly and evenly. Engineered wood and floors that sat in water for a long time are less forgiving.

The one rule that saves the most floors is this: do not sand, seal or refinish until the wood and the subfloor have reached normal moisture readings. Sanding wet wood locks in trouble, and a fresh finish over trapped moisture will peel or cloud.

What to do in the first two days

  1. Stop the water source and extract any standing water.
  2. Lift rugs, mats and furniture so air and drying equipment can reach the boards.
  3. Photograph the floor from several angles, so you can show how it changed.
  4. Do not cover the floor with plastic, towels or wax, because covers hold moisture in.
  5. Do not walk on lifting boards in heels or boots, since wet wood dents easily.
  6. Call a crew to meter the floor before you try to heat or fan it dry.

Wet hardwood is fragile in a way dry wood is not. Boards that would shrug off foot traffic can chip, split or pop at the tongue when saturated. Light, careful use of the room is fine until the crew arrives.

Cupping versus crowning

Cupping means the edges of each board rise higher than the center, so the board looks like a shallow dish. It happens when the bottom of the board is wetter than the top, which is the usual result of a leak from below or a wet subfloor.

Crowning is the opposite. The center of the board rises above the edges. It can appear after cupped floors are sanded too early, or when the top surface got wetter than the underside. Both are signs of moisture imbalance, and neither one is a finish problem.

How a crew dries a hardwood floor

After extraction, a crew places moisture meters on boards and into the subfloor. Thermal imaging cameras help spot wet zones across the room, but they do not measure moisture. The meters do that, and the crew records readings so it knows when to stop.

Hardwood is dried with floor drying mats, panels or tented systems that pull water out through the face of the board while air movers and LGR dehumidifiers manage the room. Our water mitigation service runs these systems and takes readings daily. Drying hardwood usually takes longer than carpet, often a week or more, because wood releases water slowly.

Rushing the process with heat or a few strong fans can crack boards. Slow, even drying keeps the wood flatter.

Check the subfloor before you plan anything

The boards are only the top layer. Under them sits plywood or planks, and under that sits a crawl space or slab. If the subfloor stays damp, new boards will cup again within months.

Your crew should measure subfloor moisture from below when a crawl space is accessible, and through the boards when it is not. If a slab is under the floor, a moisture test on the concrete also matters. Soft spots, a musty smell, or fastener lines that show rust are warnings that the subfloor needs work before any finishing.

Solid wood has a thick wear layer and can often be sanded more than once. Engineered boards have a thin top veneer, so sanding a swollen board may cut through it. A repair contractor should check the thickness at a cut edge before promising anything.

Finishes, matching and what to ask about

  • Ask whether the new boards will be stained to match the old, and by whom.
  • Ask how long the finish needs to cure before furniture goes back.
  • Ask whether the whole room will be refinished so the sheen looks even.
  • Ask for the moisture readings before sanding begins.
  • Ask whether the crew follows ANSI/IICRC S500 for drying.

Patching a few boards is common, but blending the new area into the old requires sanding the full run to a wall or a doorway. Hardwood floor questions

How long should I wait before sanding a water-damaged floor?

Wait until the wood and subfloor read at normal levels, not for a set number of days. A crew with meters can tell you when. Floors often need a week or more.

Will cupped boards flatten on their own?

Mild cupping often relaxes as the floor dries evenly. Severe cupping or buckling often stays and needs repair.

Can I use a dehumidifier and fans at home?

They help with room humidity, but they do not replace controlled drying with mats and moisture checks. Without readings you will not know whether the subfloor dried.

Does insurance pay for hardwood floor repair?

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while long-term leaks often are not. Check your policy and ask your insurer, and avoid permanent repairs until the adjuster has looked.

Do Loma Linda homes with older plank floors need anything special?

Ask for a thickness check at a cut edge or a floor vent opening before choosing refinishing. Older floors may have thicker boards, but that is not true of every home.
